# Fieldnotes journal surface

## Scope and visitor mode

Redesign the journal index and article pages. Read: help readers discover and
understand an editorial note, then navigate back to the journal.

## Reader and task

Independent designers and design-minded readers arrive curious about ideas,
objects, and details shaping everyday life. They scan the latest writing, open
an essay, read comfortably, and return to the index.

## Content and constraints

Use the existing article and copy as the evidence; do not invent proof or
replace editorial claims. Preserve static Astro routes, the published-post
filter, article metadata, and working navigation.

## Unresolved decisions

No external photography, testimonials, or additional published articles are
available. Keep the current content-led experience complete without them.

## Direction contract

THESIS: Make each visit feel like opening a working color-proof book, not another
neutral blog feed; the page's strong frame must always give way to the essay.

OWN-WORLD: Set oversized editorial type against confident color plates, with
small proof-register details and exact alignment marks. Keep article prose on a
quiet, highly legible paper ground and a narrow measure.

STORY: Readers recognize the journal, find the newest note and its subject at a
glance, follow it into a comfortable essay, and can return to the index at any
point.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Keep the wordmark and familiar Journal/Contact links in the
masthead. Below, a large two-part journal headline occupies the left of an
asymmetrical proof plate; a compact swatch register sits opposite. Put the
latest-writing heading and the first oversized, numbered editorial entry
immediately below the fold line. On an article route, place the return link,
category, title, description, and date in the strong frame before the calm
reading column.

FORM: Color Proof Book, candidate 7 of seven grounded directions, derived from
printed color-proof books and studio swatch archives; seed key f3bbfd94. Its
signature interaction is a registration mark that extends into a short color
rail on a post link's hover or keyboard focus; motion settles quickly and is
removed for reduced-motion preferences.
